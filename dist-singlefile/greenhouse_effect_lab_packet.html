1. Investigation Overview & Driving Question

Driving Question: How does increasing the concentration of carbon dioxide (CO₂) in an enclosed atmosphere affect the absorption of infrared thermal radiation and the rate of temperature increase over time?

In this investigation, you will compare three identical atmospheric chambers exposed to equal incandescent thermal radiation (100W heat lamp at 12 inches, clamped at 4 inches elevation):

  • Station 1 (Control): Ambient Air (~420 ppm CO₂) · 0 Effervescent Tablets
  • Station 2 (Medium CO₂): Elevated CO₂ (~1,500 ppm CO₂) · 2 Effervescent Tablets
  • Station 3 (High CO₂): Enriched CO₂ (~3,200 ppm CO₂) · 4 Effervescent Tablets

2. Pre-Lab Hypothesis

State your prediction before running the experiment (If... then... because...):

Example: If the concentration of CO₂ inside the bottle increases, then the maximum temperature reached after 15 minutes will be higher, because CO₂ molecules absorb outgoing infrared radiation...

5. Post-Lab Analysis & Inquiry Questions

Question 1: Compare the total temperature change (ΔT = Final - Initial) for each bottle. What relationship do you observe between tablet count (CO₂ concentration) and temperature rise?

Question 2: Why was it essential to keep the lamp distance (12 inches), lamp height (4 inches), water volume (100 mL), and bottle size identical across all stations?

Question 3 (Radiative Physics): Both light and infrared heat travel from the lamp into the bottles. Why does the bottle with higher CO₂ retain more heat energy than ambient air?

Question 4 (Real-World Planetary Application): Earth's natural greenhouse effect warms our planet by approximately 33°C (without it, Earth would be a frozen -18°C). How does this experiment demonstrate the impact of human fossil fuel emissions on planetary energy balance?

Teacher Facilitator & Answer Key Reference

Benchmark Values (15-Minute Data Curve):

  • Station 1 (0 Tablets · Ambient ~420 ppm): 0m: 21.0°C → 3m: 21.6°C → 6m: 22.2°C → 9m: 22.7°C → 12m: 23.1°C → 15m: 23.5°C (ΔT = +2.5°C)
  • Station 2 (2 Tablets · Medium ~1,500 ppm): 0m: 21.0°C → 3m: 22.1°C → 6m: 23.3°C → 9m: 24.3°C → 12m: 25.2°C → 15m: 25.9°C (ΔT = +4.9°C)
  • Station 3 (4 Tablets · High ~3,200 ppm): 0m: 21.0°C → 3m: 22.8°C → 6m: 24.5°C → 9m: 26.0°C → 12m: 27.3°C → 15m: 28.4°C (ΔT = +7.4°C)

Key Concept Takeaway: CO₂ molecules possess asymmetric molecular vibrational dipole modes at 15 micrometers (infrared spectrum). When the bottle surfaces re-emit thermal radiation as infrared, higher concentrations of CO₂ capture and re-radiate this heat internally, slowing cooling and establishing a significantly higher radiative thermal equilibrium.
